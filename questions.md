# Open Questions

## On Simon's Actual Position

1. **Does Simon apply his framework consistently to all resources?**
   He uses copper and energy as primary examples. Does he make the same
   argument about water, arable land, biodiversity? These seem harder to
   reduce to "services" with indefinite substitutability. I was unable to
   find evidence on this from the sources read so far.

2. **Does Simon address non-substitutable resources?**
   Some ecological services (pollination, climate regulation, topsoil
   formation) may not be amenable to his services-based redefinition.
   Did he engage with this critique? His dismissal of entropy concerns
   suggests he might, but I haven't found a direct treatment.

3. **Where does the "Simonism" framing originate?**
   The research question uses the term "Simonism." Is this a term used
   in the literature, or is it an informal coinage? Boudreaux's articles
   are the closest published articulation of the strong thesis, but they
   don't use the term.

## On the Wikipedia Characterization

4. **Is the Wikipedia conflation accidental or structural?**
   Is the problem just poor editing, or does the secondary literature
   itself routinely conflate Simon's position with generic cornucopianism?
   If the latter, the Wikipedia article is reflecting a widespread
   misunderstanding, not inventing one.

5. **Who else draws this distinction?**
   Are there academic papers or books that distinguish "Simonism" from
   "cornucopianism"? The distinction seems important but I haven't found
   scholarly treatment of it specifically.

## Potential Weaknesses of Simon's Position

6. **Is the services redefinition question-begging?**
   If you redefine "resource" as "service," you've moved the goalpost.
   The question "will we run out of copper?" becomes "will we always have
   electrical conductivity?" — but this arguably just pushes the finiteness
   question to a different level. Is there a finite set of possible
   physical mechanisms for providing a given service?

7. **Does the open-system framing have empirical content?**
   Simon argues the system is "open" — but is this a testable claim or
   a philosophical commitment? If it can't be falsified, it has the same
   problem he attributes to finiteness claims.
